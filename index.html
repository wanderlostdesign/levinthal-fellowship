The Fellowship

Interested in tech, entrepreneurship & venture capital?

The Levinthal Fellowship gives a small group of BYU students the chance to learn directly from experienced entrepreneurs and investors, then put that learning to work inside a real startup.

It's hosted by Mike Levinthal, who spent four decades in venture capital, including years as Managing Partner at Mayfield Fund, one of Silicon Valley's oldest firms. Today he runs Levinthal Capital and has been an early investor in companies like Cotopaxi and Peloton. Fellows spend the Winter semester in his seminar, work remotely with a Mexico City startup, and then join that company in person for a month.

The best way to understand how startups are built is to sit across the table from the people who build and fund them.

The Program

Three parts, one cohort.

The fellowship counts as a 3-credit course. It runs from January through late May 2027.

I · The Seminar

The Levinthal Seminar

Winter 2027 · Tuesday evenings

A discussion seminar in the tradition of Harvard Business School's case method. There are no lectures. Fellows read the material, come prepared to argue a position, and work through real decisions founders and investors faced. Founders and VCs from Mike's network join as guests, and some sessions are held at his home in Park City.

II · Remote Internship

Join the team early

During the semester · ~5 hours a week

Each fellow is matched with a startup in Mexico City and starts contributing remotely while the seminar is in session. You'll learn the business, meet the team, and arrive in May already knowing the work.

III · Mexico City

A month on the ground

April 25 – May 25, 2027

Fellows live in Mexico City and work in person at their startup for a full month. You'll be inside one of Latin America's fastest-growing tech ecosystems alongside the rest of the cohort.

A Proven Model

Built on the Mayfield Fellows tradition.

In the 1990s Mike Levinthal helped bring Mayfield Fund behind a new Stanford program that paired a small cohort of students with coursework in technology ventures and a hands-on startup internship. The Mayfield Fellows Program has now run for three decades, and it later expanded to UC Berkeley. Its alumni have founded and led companies across Silicon Valley.

The Levinthal Fellowship brings that model to BYU, pairing a small cohort and real mentorship with an internship placement in Mexico City.

Ciudad de México

Why Mexico City.

Mexico City has become one of the most dynamic startup hubs in Latin America. Venture capital is flowing in, founders are building for hundreds of millions of customers, and young companies hand interns real responsibility.

Fellows see firsthand how startups are built in an emerging market, and they come home with a network that spans two countries.
